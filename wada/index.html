Half Awake

A Within-Brain Comparison of Aperiodic and Oscillatory EEG During Unilateral Methohexital Anesthesia

Primary bipolar EEG analysis of a rare within-participant hemispheric perturbation

View Full PosterComing soon Explore the Analysis Contact Luis

Primary result

The result, at a glance

In a frozen cohort of 27 participants, the standard-bipolar aperiodic exponent showed a positive injected-minus-contralateral interaction.

The mean interaction was 0.245 exponent units (95% CI 0.043–0.447; raw two-sided p=.019) in the primary standard-bipolar analysis.

The question

One perturbation, two hemispheres

A concise map from the clinical procedure to the analysis contrast.

  1. 01

    Unilateral anesthesia

    Methohexital is administered through one carotid artery to produce temporary, predominantly unilateral cerebral anesthesia.

  2. 02

    Two conditions

    EEG is compared between baseline and anesthesia within each hemisphere of the same participant.

  3. 03

    Two hemispheres

    Baseline-to-anesthesia change is estimated separately for the injected and contralateral sides.

  4. 04

    Difference of differences

    The contralateral change is subtracted from the injected-side change to test for a lateralized interaction.

  5. 05

    Candidate features

    The same bipolar contrast is applied to aperiodic exponent, theta peak strength, and descriptive aperiodic offset.

Within-brain design

Each participant provides a simultaneous comparison

Each participant contributes baseline and anesthesia measurements from both hemispheres. The principal interaction is [(anesthesia − baseline) injected] − [(anesthesia − baseline) contralateral]. The contralateral hemisphere is a simultaneous within-participant comparator, not an untreated or unaffected control.

What signal is spatially specific?

Does the aperiodic EEG exponent change more after unilateral methohexital administration in the injected hemisphere than in the contralateral hemisphere, and does oscillatory theta peak strength show the same pattern?

A rare natural experiment

The Wada procedure temporarily perturbs one hemisphere while EEG is recorded from both sides, enabling a within-participant comparison.

A challenging clinical signal

Movement, muscle activity, arousal, and behavioral testing can create broadband changes that are shared across hemispheres.

Background and peaks

Spectral parameterization separates the aperiodic background from detected oscillatory peaks instead of treating every power shift as rhythmic activity.

Lateralization, not an untreated control

The injected-minus-contralateral interaction asks whether change follows the injected side; it does not assume the other hemisphere is unaffected.

01 / Design

From clinical EEG to a within-brain contrast

The current pipeline centers the predefined standard-bipolar montage and a paired, within-participant interaction.

Clinical context

Wada procedure

Methohexital is administered through one carotid artery to produce temporary, predominantly unilateral cerebral anesthesia while EEG is recorded from both hemispheres.

Signal preparation

EEG preprocessing

A fixed 23-channel scalp set is retained after Fp1, Fp2, and nonstandard channels are removed. Supported 512-Hz recordings are resampled to 256 Hz; continuous data are demeaned, linearly detrended, and high-pass filtered at 0.5 Hz.

Quality control

Artifact handling

Recordings use either approved manual clean segments or an objective 4-second epoch gate based on robust and absolute peak-to-peak amplitude. Standard conditions require at least 15 accepted epochs; curated conditions require at least 50 seconds.

Primary montage

Standard bipolar signals

A predefined 12-pair bipolar montage is derived from cleaned continuous data before epoch construction. Each signal is the first electrode minus the second, and pair laterality is aligned to the injected side.

Primary region

Tier-1 bipolar ROI

The primary parasagittal region uses F3–C3 and C3–P3 on the left and F4–C4 and C4–P4 on the right. The injection side determines which pair set is labeled injected or contralateral.

Analysis windows

Paired accepted segments

The source baseline spans up to 180 seconds before injection and ends 10 seconds before it; anesthesia source data begin 15 seconds after injection. Accepted whole segments are accumulated toward a 100-second target without splitting boundary segments.

Spectral estimator

Multitaper spectra

FieldTrip DPSS multitaper spectra use 2-Hz smoothing, a 0.5-Hz frequency grid, and 180-second padding. Spectra are estimated per accepted segment and averaged in linear power using segment duration as the weight.

Spectral model

Aperiodic and periodic features

Fixed-mode FOOOF fitting separates aperiodic exponent and offset from detected peaks. Fits use 2–35 Hz or 2–25 Hz according to acquisition hardware, with theta defined as the strongest accepted peak from 4–8 Hz.

Primary inference

Within-participant group test

The primary exponent result uses a two-sided paired t test of the injected-minus-contralateral interaction. The displayed p-value is raw, and the saved aggregate result does not provide a standardized effect size.

02 / Findings

Primary bipolar findings

Aggregate results distinguish the primary exponent interaction from oscillatory and descriptive comparison measures.

Primary analysis · standard bipolar

Aperiodic exponent showed an injected-side interaction

After anesthesia, the aperiodic exponent increased more on the injected side than on the contralateral side in the standard-bipolar analysis.

N=27 · mean interaction=0.245 · 95% CI 0.043–0.447 · t(26)=2.49 · raw two-sided p=.019

The primary result is a two-sided paired t test of the injected-minus-contralateral interaction; the displayed p-value is raw and no standardized effect size is available in the saved aggregate result.

The primary bipolar analysis supports a positive lateralized exponent change.

Caution: This result does not by itself establish a validated clinical biomarker or causal mechanism.

Oscillatory comparison

Theta did not show the same interaction

Theta peak strength changed little on either side and did not show evidence of an injected-versus-contralateral difference.

N=27 · mean interaction=−0.0042 · raw two-sided p=.852 · confidence interval not available

Theta was evaluated as a comparison endpoint using the same injected-minus-contralateral logic; its saved aggregate result does not include a confidence interval.

The current cohort does not provide statistical evidence for a theta peak-strength interaction.

Caution: An injected-side theta peak was detected in only 6 of 27 participants, and nondetection is encoded as zero.

Descriptive broadband feature

Aperiodic offset showed a positive direction

The bipolar aperiodic offset had a positive average injected-minus-contralateral interaction, but it was not tested inferentially.

N=27 · mean interaction=0.721 · SD=0.478 · 26 positive / 1 negative · no inferential p-value

Offset was summarized descriptively; no current hypothesis test, confidence interval, or multiplicity correction is available.

The direction is notable as a descriptive broadband-power pattern, not as confirmed statistical evidence.

Caution: Offset is scale- and broadband-power-sensitive and cannot be assigned a movement-specific mechanism from this analysis.

Descriptive signal availability

Exponent estimates were more consistently available

An exponent estimate was returned for every fitted participant, while an injected-side theta peak was detected much less often.

N=27 · exponent fits=27/27 · injected-side theta detected=6/27 · descriptive only

Detectability was summarized as an availability measure and was not an inferential endpoint.

The fitted exponent yields a continuous estimate more often than the peak-detection rule identifies theta.

Caution: Availability is not accuracy, validity, diagnostic performance, or proof that one feature is biologically superior.
